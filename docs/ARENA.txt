===============================================================
100. STADIUM / ARENA — OVERALL DESIGN
=====================================

Create a large, believable modern horse-racing stadium designed specifically for a multiplayer 3D party game.

The stadium should look like a REAL purpose-built horse-racing venue, not a football stadium, soccer stadium, generic arena, or simple circular grandstand.

The overall architectural language should match the established reference design:

* Light gray concrete structural construction
* Dark gray structural beams
* Blue metal railings
* Blue, green, and yellow seating
* Large open-air spectator concourses
* Multiple levels of seating
* Large staircases between levels
* Covered upper seating
* Large stadium floodlights
* Large central scoreboard
* Concession stands
* Betting areas
* Player-accessible walkways
* Horse tunnel
* Fenced player interaction ring
* Central landscaped infield

The stadium should feel substantial.

It should look large enough to hold a significant crowd while still being compact enough to function as a multiplayer game map.

The stadium should have a strong visual hierarchy:

CENTER:
The horse-racing track.

AROUND THE TRACK:
The player interaction ring and safety barriers.

OUTSIDE THE RING:
The spectator seating and stadium concourses.

ABOVE THE SEATING:
The upper floors, balconies, structural supports, and roofs.

OUTSIDE/BEHIND THE STANDS:
Entrances, exits, service areas, concession access, stairs, and circulation.

The stadium should have a slightly stylized, clean 3D-game appearance while maintaining believable real-world architecture.

Avoid excessive realism that would make the environment visually noisy.

Use large, readable shapes and clear architectural forms.

===============================================================
101. STADIUM SHAPE AND FOOTPRINT
================================

The stadium footprint should generally follow the oval shape of the racing track.

It should NOT be a perfect circle.

The long axis of the stadium should run along the two long straight sections of the horse track.

The short axis should run across the two curved ends.

The grandstands should follow the curvature of the track.

However, the stadium does NOT need to be perfectly symmetrical.

Different portions of the stadium can have different architectural heights and layouts.

This asymmetry is intentional.

Some sections should contain:

* One seating floor
* Two seating floors
* Three seating floors

At least TWO major sections should have THREE full seating floors.

Other sections should have one or two floors.

This creates the distinctive stepped skyline visible in the reference design.

The stadium should feel as though it was expanded and developed into multiple architectural sections rather than generated as one perfectly repetitive structure.

===============================================================
102. REQUIRED CAMERA CONSISTENCY
================================

The entire stadium must represent ONE physical building.

Create the model so that the following views show the same architecture:

* Front
* Back
* Left
* Right
* Isometric
* High isometric
* Top-down
* Player eye-level
* Track-level

Do not redesign the stadium for different views.

If a three-floor grandstand is visible from the front, that same three-floor grandstand must exist when viewing the stadium from the back.

Stairs, roofs, entrances, tunnels, seating sections, concession areas, and structural supports must all physically occupy consistent locations.

===============================================================
103. HORSE RACING TRACK
=======================

The main feature of the stadium is one continuous oval dirt horse-racing track.

The track should occupy most of the central stadium footprint.

The racing surface should be a warm brown/tan dirt color.

It should have subtle visible tire/hoof marks and slightly darker worn racing lines, but remain clean and readable.

The track must have:

* Two long straightaways
* Two large rounded turns
* Inner boundary
* Outer boundary
* Starting area
* Finish line
* Starting gate
* Track signage
* Safety barriers
* Fence posts
* Inner rail
* Outer rail

The track must be wide enough for several horses to race side-by-side.

The turns should have a realistic radius rather than sharp corners.

The track should have a slight banking or visual shaping toward the outside where appropriate, but should remain simple enough for a game.

===============================================================
104. TRACK RAILINGS
===================

Use continuous white/light-gray racing rails around the track.

The rails should consist of:

* Repeating vertical posts
* Horizontal rails
* Regular spacing
* Rounded or clean rectangular metal sections

There should be an inner rail separating the racing surface from the central infield.

There should be an outer rail separating the racing surface from the player interaction area.

The rails should be physically continuous around the oval except at designated openings.

The player-side outer barrier should be strong enough to clearly communicate:

"Players cannot enter the racing track."

===============================================================
105. STARTING GATE
==================

Place a recognizable horse-racing starting gate on one of the straight sections.

The starting gate should contain multiple individual horse stalls.

Each stall should have:

* Metal frame
* Front gate
* Rear gate
* Stall divider
* Numbering
* Wheels/supports
* Overhead structure

The gate should look like equipment that could realistically be moved around the track.

It should be large enough to accommodate the game's maximum number of racing horses.

===============================================================
106. FINISH LINE
================

Create a clearly recognizable finish line on the main straightaway.

Include:

* Painted finish markings
* Checkered or racing-style markings
* Finish-line posts
* Track-side signage
* Camera equipment
* Optional timing equipment

The finish line should be visible from the majority of the main spectator sections.

===============================================================
107. CENTRAL INFIELD
====================

The center of the oval track contains a large landscaped grass infield.

The infield should NOT simply be an empty green rectangle.

It should contain:

* Grass
* Curved landscaping
* Small trees
* Shrubs
* Decorative hedges
* Walking paths
* Small maintenance structures
* Decorative flower beds
* Central pond/water feature
* Fountain

The landscaping should follow the oval geometry.

Keep the center visually open enough that players can easily see the horses.

===============================================================
108. CENTRAL POND / WATER FEATURE
=================================

Place a decorative pond approximately in the central portion of the infield.

The pond should have:

* Organic oval/rounded shape
* Blue water
* Small fountain
* Concrete/stone border
* Surrounding grass
* Decorative landscaping

The pond is primarily visual.

It should not interfere with the horse track.

===============================================================
109. CENTRAL SCOREBOARD
=======================

Place a large electronic scoreboard in the infield.

The scoreboard should be mounted on a substantial support structure.

It should face the primary spectator seating.

Include:

* Large rectangular screen
* Thick dark frame
* Support columns
* Race information
* Horse numbers
* Odds
* Current race position
* Finish results

The screen should be large enough to see from the upper seating levels.

The scoreboard should not completely block the view of the track.

===============================================================
110. PLAYER INTERACTION RING
============================

Immediately outside the racing track is a dedicated player-accessible interaction ring.

This is one of the most important areas of the entire map.

The ring follows the oval shape of the track.

It sits between:

TRACK → PLAYER RING → STADIUM

The player ring should be a wide paved/concrete walkway.

Players can walk around the entire stadium from this area.

The ring should contain:

* Concrete pavement
* Safety railings
* Viewing locations
* Benches where appropriate
* Trash cans
* Small decorative objects
* Signs
* Concessions
* Item booths
* Access to betting areas
* Access to staircases

The ring must be physically separated from the horse track.

Players cannot normally walk onto the racing surface.

===============================================================
111. PLAYER INTERACTION FENCE
=============================

The boundary between the track and player ring should use a strong stadium-style fence.

Use:

* Blue/dark metal posts
* Horizontal rails
* Vertical supports
* Concrete mounting points

The fence should be approximately waist/chest height for the player characters.

It must be low enough that players can clearly see the horses.

It must be high enough that players cannot accidentally walk onto the track.

Certain designated sections can contain slightly wider viewing areas where players can stand and watch races.

===============================================================
112. MAIN STADIUM SEATING
=========================

Spectator seating surrounds the majority of the oval.

The seats should NOT look like long flat benches.

Use individual molded stadium seats.

Each seat should have:

* Individual seat pan
* Backrest
* Small side spacing
* Narrow supporting structure
* Slightly curved back
* Mounted connection to the concrete platform

The seats should be arranged in long curved rows that follow the stadium.

Each row should contain many individual seats.

There should be clear aisles separating seating blocks.

Seats should be slightly angled toward the racing track.

Seats around the curved ends should rotate progressively toward the track so that spectators naturally face the racing surface.

===============================================================
113. SEATING COLORS
===================

Seat colors identify the floor level.

This color system MUST remain consistent throughout the stadium.

1ST FLOOR:

YELLOW / GOLD

These are the lowest spectator seats.

2ND FLOOR:

GREEN

These are the middle-level spectator seats.

3RD FLOOR:

BLUE

These are the highest spectator seats.

The colors should not represent teams.

They represent architectural elevation.

The color scheme should be highly visible from a distance.

Avoid randomly mixing the three colors within a single floor.

Each floor should primarily use its designated color.

===============================================================
114. FIRST FLOOR SEATING
========================

The first floor is closest to the player interaction ring.

It should generally contain the largest number of easily accessible seats.

The first-floor seating should have:

* Yellow/gold individual seats
* Multiple rows
* Concrete seating platforms
* Short stair aisles
* Blue/dark handrails
* Walkways behind the seating
* Access to the player ring
* Access to concessions
* Access to betting areas

The first-floor seating should be relatively low.

Players should feel close to the horses.

Some sections can have only this single seating level.

These one-floor sections create visual variation in the stadium.

===============================================================
115. SECOND FLOOR SEATING
=========================

Two-floor and three-floor sections should have a second level above the first.

The second floor contains:

* Green individual seats
* Multiple rows
* Concrete structural platform
* Walkways
* Railings
* Stair access
* Supporting columns
* Open concourse areas

The second-floor seating should be elevated enough to provide a noticeably better view of the entire track.

The front of the second floor should have a sturdy safety railing.

The second floor should not simply float above the first floor.

It must have visible:

* Concrete supports
* Columns
* Beams
* Structural walls

===============================================================
116. THIRD FLOOR SEATING
========================

At least TWO major grandstand sections should contain a third seating floor.

The third floor contains:

* Blue individual seats
* Multiple rows
* Concrete platform
* Walkways
* Blue/dark safety railings
* Stair access
* Structural supports
* Large roof structure above

The third floor should be the highest seating area.

It should provide a panoramic view over the entire stadium.

The third floor should be visibly taller than the lower levels.

===============================================================
117. SEATING ROW GEOMETRY
=========================

Rows should rise gradually toward the rear.

Do not make every row sit at the exact same elevation.

Each successive row should be slightly higher.

This creates realistic stadium sightlines.

There should be enough vertical difference between rows that players/NPCs in the rear can see over those in front.

Each row should have a narrow horizontal walkway or step structure as appropriate.

Avoid seats intersecting with:

* Concrete
* Railings
* Stairs
* Walls
* Other seats

===============================================================
118. SEATING BLOCKS / AISLES
============================

Divide the grandstands into clearly separated seating blocks.

Each block should contain several rows.

Between blocks, create stair aisles.

The aisles should:

* Run vertically through the seating
* Have concrete steps
* Have blue/dark handrails
* Connect to horizontal walkways
* Lead toward concourses

Do not create impossibly long uninterrupted seating rows.

The stadium should have a rhythm of:

SEATS → AISLE → SEATS → AISLE → SEATS

===============================================================
119. STADIUM STAIRCASES
=======================

The stadium staircases should be substantial architectural elements.

Use the previously established staircase design as the reference.

The staircase should consist of:

* Gray concrete steps
* Gray concrete side walls
* Blue metal handrails
* Blue vertical railing posts
* Rectangular landings
* Structural supports
* Clean, slightly stylized geometry

The steps should be individual rectangular slabs rather than one smooth ramp.

The staircases should be wide enough for multiple players/NPCs to pass.

Use approximately consistent step proportions throughout the stadium.

REFERENCE STAIR DIMENSIONS:

* Stair width: approximately 8 game units
* Stair total depth: approximately 10 game units
* Total rise: approximately 12 game units
* Approximately 8 major steps per flight
* Step height: approximately 1.5 game units
* Step depth: approximately 1.25 game units
* Handrail height: approximately 4.5 game units

These dimensions can be adjusted slightly for gameplay, but the stairs should retain the same visual proportions.

===============================================================
120. STAIRCASE HANDRAILS
========================

Handrails should use the stadium's blue metal design.

Each handrail should have:

* Vertical support posts
* Horizontal/diagonal top rail
* Rounded or beveled ends
* Strong attachment points
* Consistent spacing

The handrails should follow the incline of the staircase.

At landings, the handrails should become horizontal.

The handrails should not abruptly terminate in midair.

===============================================================
121. STAIRCASE LANDINGS
=======================

Long staircases should contain logical landings.

Landings should provide:

* Space for players to stop
* Connection to horizontal walkways
* Access to seating sections
* Access to other stairs

Where necessary, stairs can use switchback configurations.

Avoid enormous uninterrupted flights that look impractical.

===============================================================
122. VERTICAL STADIUM CIRCULATION
=================================

The stadium needs a believable circulation system.

Players should be able to understand:

GROUND LEVEL
↓
1ST FLOOR
↓
2ND FLOOR
↓
3RD FLOOR

without getting lost.

Stairs should be placed at regular intervals around the stadium.

Three-floor sections should have obvious vertical circulation.

One-floor sections should not have unnecessary giant stair structures.

===============================================================
123. GRANDSTAND STRUCTURE
=========================

Behind and beneath the seating, create believable structural architecture.

Use:

* Large concrete columns
* Horizontal beams
* Slanted supports
* Concrete walls
* Structural platforms
* Metal railings
* Ceiling structures
* Utility areas

The stadium should look capable of physically supporting its own weight.

Do not leave seating floating in space.

Every elevated seating platform should have visible structural support.

===============================================================
124. OPEN CONCOURSES
====================

Behind the seating rows, create wide stadium concourses.

The concourses should feel like real public circulation spaces.

They should contain:

* Concrete floors
* Structural columns
* Overhead ceilings
* Lighting
* Signs
* Trash cans
* Concessions
* Betting tables
* Restrooms
* Stairs
* Railings
* Doors
* Windows/openings

Some concourses should be open toward the stadium.

Players should be able to see the track from appropriate locations.

===============================================================
125. CONCESSION AREAS
=====================

Create dedicated food and item concession areas throughout the stadium.

These should primarily be positioned along major concourses.

Each concession should be a compact storefront/booth rather than a random table.

A typical concession booth should contain:

* Rectangular counter
* Countertop
* Service window
* Large overhead sign
* Awning
* Menu board
* Food display
* Storage shelves
* Small preparation area
* Trash cans
* Queue/standing area

Use clear signage such as:

SNACKS & DRINKS

HOT DOGS

FOOD

ITEMS

The booths should use a simple stadium concession aesthetic.

They should be recognizable from a distance.

===============================================================
126. OUTDOOR FOOD / ITEM BOOTHS
===============================

Some concessions should be positioned directly along the player interaction ring.

These can be smaller outdoor stands.

Use:

* Small building
* Counter
* Awning
* Umbrella
* Menu sign
* Food display
* Trash can
* Player standing area

These booths can sell the throwable items used by players.

For example:

HOT DOGS

ITEMS

The stands should not obstruct the main walkway.

===============================================================
127. BETTING AREAS
==================

Create dedicated betting areas separate from the food concessions.

Betting areas should look like permanent horse-racing betting facilities.

Use:

* Long betting counters
* Individual betting windows
* Digital screens
* Horse-number displays
* Odds displays
* Large signs
* Player standing areas
* Queue space

Large signs should clearly communicate:

PLACE BETS HERE

The betting counters should face the player side of the concourse.

The screens can display:

HORSE NUMBER

ODDS

WIN

PLACE

RACE NUMBER

Players should have enough space to approach the betting tables without blocking circulation.

===============================================================
128. BETTING TABLE DESIGN
=========================

Individual betting stations should be visually separated.

Each station can contain:

* Counter
* Small display
* Horse/odds screen
* Ticket/payment interface
* Number marker
* Small overhead light

Multiple stations should be placed side-by-side.

The entire betting area should feel like a real betting counter rather than a collection of generic desks.

===============================================================
129. HORSE ENTRY / EXIT TUNNEL
==============================

Create a major horse tunnel connecting the track to the stadium's service/stable side.

This should be one of the most obvious functional architectural features.

The tunnel should be large enough for horses and handlers.

The opening should be significantly larger than a normal human doorway.

The tunnel should contain:

* Large rectangular/arched opening
* Concrete walls
* Heavy structural frame
* Large gate
* Track-side fencing
* Lighting
* Signage
* Service corridor
* Stable-side connection

The tunnel should open directly onto the track.

There should be enough clearance for a horse and rider/handler.

===============================================================
130. HORSE TUNNEL FUNCTION
==========================

The tunnel has two purposes.

BEFORE A RACE:

HORSES ENTER THE TRACK

AFTER A RACE:

HORSES EXIT THE TRACK

The tunnel should connect to an unseen or partially visible stable/service area behind the stadium.

Do not make the tunnel a decorative hole in a wall.

It must physically connect to a plausible backstage/service corridor.

===============================================================
131. HORSE SERVICE AREA
=======================

Behind the horse tunnel, create enough visible architecture to imply a larger stable/service complex.

Include:

* Service road
* Stable doors
* Utility buildings
* Fencing
* Equipment
* Loading/service area
* Staff access
* Horse holding area

This area does not need to be the main focus of the map.

It simply needs to make the horse tunnel believable.

===============================================================
132. STADIUM ROOFS
==================

The stadium's highest seating sections should have large protective roofs.

The roofs should resemble modern stadium grandstand roofs.

Use:

* Large horizontal roof panels
* Dark underside
* Metal structural beams
* Supporting columns
* Slight overhang
* Lighting fixtures

The roof should extend over the uppermost seating rows.

It should not completely cover the entire stadium.

The racing track and central infield remain open to the sky.

HORSE/SERVICE ACCESS

Use several towers around the oval.

===============================================================
141. PLAYER NAVIGATION
======================

Every major public area must be physically connected.

A player should be able to travel:

MAIN ENTRANCE
→ CONCOURSE
→ BETTING AREA
→ STAIRCASE
→ UPPER SEATING
→ STAIRCASE
→ PLAYER RING
→ CONCESSION
→ STADIUM EXIT

without encountering impossible gaps.

Do not create decorative areas that appear accessible but cannot actually be reached.

===============================================================
142. GAMEPLAY PRIORITY
======================

The architecture should support the game's multiplayer gameplay.

Players need clear access to:

* Seats
* Betting tables
* Concessions
* Item shops
* Player interaction ring
* Stairs
* Viewing areas
* Entrances/exits

The environment should never make important gameplay locations difficult to find.

The track must remain visible from most important spectator areas.

===============================================================
143. VISUAL STYLE
=================

The final stadium should have a polished, colorful, slightly simplified 3D game aesthetic.

Use:

* Clean geometry
* Simple materials
* Strong silhouettes
* Limited material variety
* Clear colors
* Moderate detail
* Slightly exaggerated proportions where helpful for gameplay

The stadium should look substantially more detailed than a primitive blockout, but should NOT become photorealistic.

It should feel like a high-quality multiplayer game environment.

===============================================================
144. REQUIRED OVERALL RESULT
============================

The final result should look like a large professional horse-racing stadium built around a multiplayer party-game experience.

The viewer should immediately understand:

1. Where the horses race.
2. Where the spectators sit.
3. Where the players walk.
4. Where players bet.
5. Where players buy items.
6. Where the horses enter and exit.
7. How players reach upper floors.
8. How the three seating floors are organized.
9. Which areas are public and which are restricted.
10. How the entire stadium physically connects.

The finished stadium should resemble the previously established reference concept:

A large oval dirt racetrack surrounded by layered grandstands, with yellow first-floor seating, green second-floor seating, blue third-floor seating, blue railings, gray concrete construction, large staircases, open concourses, concession booths, betting counters, a central landscaped infield with a pond and scoreboard, floodlights, and a clearly defined horse tunnel.

It should feel like a real stadium that could actually be constructed, while remaining visually clean and recognizable as a stylized multiplayer video-game environment.
